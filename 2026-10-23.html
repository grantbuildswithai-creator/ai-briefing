AI Briefing — Fri, Oct 23, 2026

Top stories

1. "Black October": AI-accelerated disclosures overwhelm defenders

Over roughly 48 hours, a cluster of critical disclosures and breaches landed together. PHP2Shell (the unauthenticated RCE in PHP covered yesterday) got company: GitLab disclosed a CVSS 10 flaw affecting releases since 2014 (patched in 19.5.1), Node.js shipped a Brotli decoder RCE fix, and NGINX/HTTP3 had DoS issues. On the AI-tooling side, LiteLLM 1.107.0 shipped with a password stealer, Railway told users to rotate secrets after possible key exposure, and Zeabur is shutting down after 321,810 accounts were breached. Certificate authority eMudhra is exiting the CA business after a researcher reportedly cracked its trivially weak keys (one was said to be the number 4) with a single Claude prompt. On the attacker side, Microsoft attributes the ConnectWise supply-chain compromise to China-aligned Storm-0940, which used the open agentic pentest framework ARTEX against 2,250+ Microsoft 365 tenants at ~2,900 sign-in attempts per minute — reaching privileged access in under 3 hours in some cases. CrowdStrike documented four operators running AI-agent campaigns, one hitting at least 15 Argentine healthcare organizations. Latent Space notes reported monthly CVEs rose from ~4k to ~15k since April.

Why it matters: The follow-through to yesterday's Patch the Planet story: AI-era vulnerability-finding has reached industrial scale. The honest debate underneath: some researchers call the "offense winning" narrative disinformation — this was a defensive audit with no evidence of large-scale exploitation — and reportedly only ~4 of the ~16 incidents actually occurred this week; the rest were older finds disclosed now. Either way, the race is now disclosure machinery vs. exploit generation, both AI-driven.

Sources: [Latent Space [AINews]](https://latent.space/p/ainews-black-october/), 🎧 AI Daily Brief

2. OpenAI's Decisions API goes GA: near-real-time decisions at up to 10x the speed of GPT-6 Luna

OpenAI made its Decisions API generally available — a small, focused API for bounded-answer decisions (classify an input, route a request, pick the next action) from text, images, and structured inputs. Since public beta it added native GPT-Live integration, prompt caching, US/EU inference residency, native JSON inputs, images in question instructions, and up to 400 questions per request. Customers report strong results: Box saw ~5x faster route selection on certificate-of-insurance cases at the same accuracy as GPT-6 Luna via the Responses API; another customer scored the risk of agent-generated actions 40x faster at p95 with comparable accuracy to frontier models; AI-cost attribution ran 4x faster and 30% cheaper.

Why it matters: Agents make thousands of tiny decisions per workflow, and latency/cost compound. A cheap, bounded-answer decision layer is infrastructure for agentic scale — and the eval numbers (same accuracy, 5–40x faster) suggest this is a real new layer, not a wrapper.

Sources: OpenAI News

3. Anthropic donates the Agent Skills standard to the Agentic AI Foundation

Anthropic donated its Agent Skills standard — the open format (a SKILL.md folder teaching agents a task or domain) it built for Claude Code — to the Agentic AI Foundation (AAIF), the Linux Foundation home it created with Block and OpenAI last year, to which it already donated MCP. More than 70 agent platforms, including ChatGPT, Cursor, GitHub Copilot, and VS Code, have integrated skills since the December 2025 publication; Anthropic cites 1 million skills published and nearly 280 million installed. The donation covers the name, logo, GitHub organization, and domain; engineers from Anthropic, Google, OpenAI, and others will maintain the standard as individuals.

Why it matters: The second major agent standard donated to neutral stewardship after MCP — the agent stack's interoperability layer is consolidating in one vendor-neutral home instead of fragmenting by vendor.

Sources: Anthropic News

4. Uber rolls out ChatGPT Work to all 30,000 employees

Uber expanded ChatGPT Work — the agent inside ChatGPT Enterprise that takes on multi-step tasks across connected tools — from a 1,000-employee pilot to its full 30,000-person global workforce, its first company-wide deployment of an agentic platform for knowledge work. It connects with Google Drive, Gmail, Google Calendar, and Slack; GPT-6 Luna is the default model. Uber pairs the rollout with an internal AI skills library, approved integrations, and employee training; its engineering teams continue using Codex for coding.

Why it matters: A real-scale data point on enterprise agentic adoption — 30k employees with an agent that completes workflows across apps, not just a chatbot, with the company publicly promising adoption and outcome data later.

Sources: OpenAI News

5. a16z renames its crypto fund "a16z Frontier," expanding to all frontier tech

Andreessen Horowitz renamed a16z Crypto to a16z Frontier, expanding the fund's scope beyond crypto startups to frontier startups broadly — companies building new technologies and creating markets that may take years to develop. Crypto investing and advocacy continue, but the framing is explicit: blockchains have receded into the background infrastructure, and the firm is re-centering on the AI wave, where "the tech is already sufficiently advanced to spawn a new generation of products and services" and native use cases — brand-new activities, not faster versions of old ones.

Why it matters: A label change as thesis statement: a16z is placing its long-horizon bet on AI-native products and markets rather than the crypto thesis alone.

Sources: a16z News, a16z.com

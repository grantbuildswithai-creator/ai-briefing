Top stories

1. OpenAI: perplexity should be the shared yardstick for frontier AI

OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki argues that perplexity — equivalently, cross-entropy loss — should become the common metric labs use to compare pretraining progress and inform shared safety standards. As a first step, OpenAI published its internal perplexity measurements: GPT-3.5 sat at ~0.99 log-perplexity on its main evaluation set (fluent conversation, the basis for the original ChatGPT), GPT-4 at ~0.91 (productive chain-of-thought reasoning), and current models now below 0.8, where transformative capabilities in cybersecurity and mathematics have emerged. Pachocki frames AI development as training a compressor on a vast corpus, with capabilities appearing as emergent, hard-to-predict consequences of pushing that one number down — and notes that publishing the figures hands competitors a clearer picture of where OpenAI stands, but that the urgency of industry-wide safety measures outweighs that concern.

Why it matters: Benchmarks saturate and go stale fast, but pretraining loss has tracked progress steadily from GPT-2 to today. If labs agree to report it, the field gets a stable, comparable signal of how fast the "intelligence substrate" is advancing — and a basis for pacing safety work before capabilities emerge, rather than reacting after they do.

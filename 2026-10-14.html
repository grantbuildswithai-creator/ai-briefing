Top stories

1. Google ships Gemini 4 Argon, its new frontier model

Google released Gemini 4 Argon globally: latest frontier model for developers, enterprises, scientists, and consumers, billed as built for "deep work" on complex multi-step tasks — coding, finance, legal, cybersecurity, and science. Pricing is $2 per million input tokens / $10 output (95% off for cached input). Google claims Argon outperforms other frontier models on an internal benchmark spanning finance, coding, legal, and tax, and leans on its own deployment receipts: separating six million lines of legacy Chromium code in weeks, cutting internal agentic code rollbacks by nearly a third, and speeding Gemma 4 E2B inference 2.85x on A100. Argon is already available in Cursor, Devin, and GitHub Copilot.

Why it matters: Independent checks are already arriving — Latent Space notes CursorBench and other benchmarks "simply independently confirm their claims," with Cursor reporting coding performance on par with Claude Opus 5.5. One caveat from Artificial Analysis: listed rates double for some deployment modes, so published cost comparisons need care. This is Google's current answer to the frontier race, and the market is treating it as a genuine contender.

Sources: Google AI Blog, [Latent Space [AINews]](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-ai-engineer-nyc-gemini-4-argon)

2. Anthropic opens a Singapore office and signs an AI-safety MOU with the Singapore government

Anthropic opened its fifth Asia-Pacific office in Singapore and signed a Memorandum of Understanding with Singapore's Ministry of Digital Development and Information, covering cooperation on AI safety, security, model evaluation, adoption research, and responsible adoption in the public and private sectors. Signed on the sidelines of Singapore International Cyber Week by co-founder Ben Mann and Permanent Secretary Kai Fong Chng, witnessed by Minister Josephine Teo. Anthropic notes Singapore ranks second of 121 countries in Claude.ai usage relative to population (~6x its size), with GovTech Singapore (1,500+ engineers on Claude Code), Grab, and DBS among local users.

Why it matters: Singapore is one of Anthropic's most active markets and a standard-setter on model testing — this is a lab formalizing a beachhead in a region where the Claude footprint is already deep, pairing commercial presence with a government safety-evaluation channel. Worth watching as a template for other APAC governments.

Sources: Anthropic News

3. Grok 4.7 lands on Amazon Bedrock, tuned for long-horizon agents

SpaceXAI's Grok 4.7 is now available on Amazon Bedrock with configurable reasoning effort, tool calling, structured output, and a 500K-token context window. Per SpaceXAI, it uses a larger base model than Grok 4.6 and a longer RL run weighted toward problems taking many hours; AWS's write-up builds a tool-calling agent and benchmarks it on three multi-step tasks (outage diagnosis, constrained maintenance scheduling, budgeted migrations), reporting 67 of 72 runs correct, with the lowest reasoning setting hitting 15 of 18 runs on roughly an eighth of the output tokens. The post walks through tuning the reasoning/cost trade-off per request.

Why it matters: Bedrock keeps widening into a multi-lab marketplace — OpenAI, Anthropic, and now xAI models side by side on managed infra. The tunable-reasoning knob is the practical story: agent builders get to dial deliberation versus token cost per request.

Sources: AWS Machine Learning Blog

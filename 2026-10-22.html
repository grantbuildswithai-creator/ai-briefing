Top stories

1. Patch the Planet: OpenAI coordinates disclosure of PHP2Shell, two critical PHP flaws

OpenAI announced Patch the Planet, a program that connects security researchers, maintainers, and incident responders to coordinate disclosure of critical vulnerabilities in widely used open source. Its first result: two critical memory-corruption flaws in PHP itself — CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, dubbed PHP2Shell — affecting a language that powers roughly 70% of the web. The flaws were found independently by Calif researcher Ryan Austin using OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol, and by Wiz (using Gemini 4 Argon, GPT-5.6 Sol, and its own Wiz Atlas tool). Researchers demonstrated pre-authentication remote code execution against WordPress, Drupal, and Joomla, plus "Heartbleed-style" remote memory disclosure against PHP applications generally. After a two-month coordinated response with PHP's maintainers, Cloudflare, and others, OpenAI shipped a PHP version checker as a Codex skill and is publishing a proposed AI-era disclosure framework — alongside a $1 billion commitment in subsidized compute for defenders, focused on under-resourced organizations protecting essential services.

Why it matters: This is the promised security-audit era arriving ahead of the defenses: frontier models found a Heartbleed-class bug before attackers did, and OpenAI says it expects findings to "increase sharply in the coming months." The race is now between disclosure machinery and exploit generation — both running on the same class of models. Latent Space's AINews notes the PHP ecosystem response was "the developer community responding in its most ideal form," but cautions the researcher RCE claims are on specific CMS demos, not a proven blanket-exploitable path.

Sources: OpenAI News · [Latent Space [AINews]](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-openais-patch-the-planet-program)

2. OpenAI models are coming to Google Cloud's Model Garden

OpenAI and Google Cloud are partnering to bring OpenAI's frontier models and Codex workflows into Google Cloud's Model Garden, purchasable through the Google Cloud Marketplace — with enterprises able to draw down existing GCP spend commitments. The pitch is regulated-industry access: Google's access controls, data governance, and infrastructure around OpenAI's models. Models will also surface in Google Antigravity and the Gemini Agent. OpenAI notes Microsoft remains its primary cloud partner.

Why it matters: The model-distribution layer keeps fracturing: frontier capability is increasingly bought through whichever cloud commit a company already holds, not through a lab's own channel. For enterprises, that's procurement convenience; for OpenAI, it's another route to workloads that already live on GCP — and a further step in the Microsoft/Google split of its enterprise surface.

Sources: OpenAI News

3. OpenAI, PerturbAI, and Scripps deploy thousands of GPT-Rosalind agents on brain-cell data

OpenAI, PerturbAI, and Scripps Research ran thousands of AI agents powered by GPT-Rosalind in parallel over the Tahoe 100M dataset — gene-disruption effects across millions of mouse-brain cells — each agent investigating a disruption, analyzing cellular responses, searching the literature, and proposing explanations. The combined output was a "reasoning atlas" linking genes, cellular effects, publications, and hypotheses in 32 minutes, surfacing unexpected biological connections relevant to Alzheimer's and Parkinson's research directions.

Why it matters: Yesterday's Orchestra Bio acquisition was the platform bet; this is the demonstration: agentic swarms compressing the literature-connection step of biology, which has always been the human-scale bottleneck. The open question is validation — leads are cheap, wet-lab confirmation is not — but the "32 minutes" number will be the one every drug program now asks to beat.

Sources: OpenAI News

4. ChatGPT Ads expands to 26 more countries; 100,000+ advertisers in eight months

ChatGPT Ads is now available in 89 countries total, adding 26 including Argentina, Chile, Colombia, Peru, Nigeria, South Africa, Serbia, Ukraine, Cambodia, Bangladesh, and Pakistan, following earlier launches in Mexico and Brazil. OpenAI says over 100,000 advertisers have run campaigns since launch roughly eight months ago, against 1.2 billion weekly users. Ads remain confined to the Free and Go plans; Plus, Pro, and Enterprise stay ad-free.

Why it matters: The conversion business is scaling fast — 100k advertisers in eight months is a real ad platform, not an experiment. The ad-free paid tiering is the balancing act: monetize the free majority without pushing the users who pay. Watch whether ad-supported usage starts shaping product behavior (prompts optimized for commercial intent) — that would be the quiet second-order effect.

Sources: OpenAI News

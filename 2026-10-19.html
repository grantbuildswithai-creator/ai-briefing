1. Anthropic commits 5% of its shares to a new Public Benefit Fund

On Sunday, Oct 18, Anthropic announced the Anthropic Public Benefit Fund, a new entity that will receive 5% of the company's shares (subject to stockholder approval). The stake can grow: the board may add another 2% for every 50% increase in the company's value above a set baseline. The fund is structured as a wholly-owned LLC rather than a 501(c)(3) charity — deliberately, for flexibility between kinds of giving: large donations to traditional nonprofits or direct distributions to specific populations. None of its equity may be used for lobbying or political giving, in the US or abroad.

Why it matters: This is a first-of-its-kind mission-locking structure from a frontier lab: the share of the company dedicated to public benefit *increases* as the company grows, and Anthropic framed it as a deliberate inversion of tech's escalating executive stock awards — "we are instead directing that equity to the public benefit." Combined with the co-founders' pledge to donate 80% of their equity and the employee equity-match program (about half of staff participate), roughly 20% of Anthropic's shares are now dedicated to the public good. The announcement names labor-market impacts of AI as the flagship problem — Anthropic has already committed $200M to studying them but says "bolder approaches may be needed." The timing also matters: it lands amid what Amodei has called a "crisis of trust" around the AI industry, and ahead of a reported IPO as soon as November (per AP) that could be the largest ever for a public benefit corporation. Open questions: who decides what gets funded — governance details are promised "in the coming months" — and the valuation milestones are still undisclosed.

Sources: Anthropic · TechCrunch · AP via The Hindu BusinessLine · Dealroom

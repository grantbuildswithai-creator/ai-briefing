AI Briefing — Wed, Nov 25, 2026

Top stories

1. Gemini 4 Neon puts Google DeepMind back in the frontier race

Google DeepMind's Gemini 4 Neon is out, and the early numbers are strong: it beats OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Astra and Anthropic's Claude Fable on LiveBench, and cut one developer's TypeScript/Rust benchmark runtime by 48.6%. Adoption is surging — Neon set a Day-1 usage record on Google's paid APIs, Perplexity has already run more than 120B Neon tokens (doubling daily volume in three days), and it's now available to Google Workspace customers through the Gemini app. Perplexity's CEO places Neon "between Opus and Fable" on intelligence, but best on response style.

Why it matters: After a stretch in which Gemini 3 disappointed and observers wrote DeepMind off as a frontier lab, Neon is a credible re-entry — and the usage figures suggest developers are switching in practice, not just on benchmarks. The open question is whether the strong first week holds once the novelty fades and the model faces sustained, harder workloads.

Sources: Latent Space

2. AI models reportedly crack two landmark math problems — verification still pending

Two extraordinary claims surfaced this week, both unconfirmed. An Anthropic GitHub repo reportedly contains a proof by an internal Claude research model that VP ≠ VNP — the algebraic analogue of P vs NP, open since 1979 — and OpenAI posted its own proof, which it says was "obtained on November 6, 2026." Separately, claims circulated that Claude helped prove the Riemann hypothesis; the mathematician behind the Lean code release said the "250,000+ pages" figure was a joke and described the code as compiling but rough. Neither lab named a model or published a formal announcement.

Why it matters: If either proof holds up under peer review, it would be the most significant AI-assisted mathematics result ever — a machine settling a problem humans have failed at for decades. But "reportedly" is doing heavy lifting here: no independent verification has been published yet, and the Riemann claim in particular looks more like a formalization artifact than a settled proof. Treat both as watch-this-space, not settled history.

Sources: AI Daily Brief, Latent Space

3. GPT-6 models design proteins from reasoning alone — and lab tests show they bind

OpenAI released a preprint showing its GPT-6 models can design new protein shapes and amino-acid sequences using only reasoning and code, without calling specialized biology models. In lab tests with Onava, proteins based on GPT-6.2's designs showed evidence of binding across five targets; 14 of 107 resequenced proteins bound, and most redesigned antibodies retained binding. Researchers could steer designs by prompting where a protein should attach and what shape it should take.

Why it matters: Protein design has been the domain of specialized models that each need expert handling. A general-purpose model that can do the job by being told what to build in plain language lowers the skill barrier dramatically — and raises the stakes, since the same capability that designs a therapeutic antibody is, in principle, the capability that designs a harmful one.

Sources: OpenAI News

4. The Nvidia fallout isn't over: House chairs say reinstatement settles nothing

Jensen Huang's reinstatement as Nvidia CEO (covered yesterday) has not closed the affair. House committee chairs say the board's decision does not resolve the smuggling and diversion questions, and Huang and board member Tim Teter will give transcribed interviews on Dec 1–2. Meanwhile the AI Daily Brief frames the week's other thread — Anthropic's IPO and the Nvidia scandal — as a backlash over who profits from AI and who gets to call the shots; Axios reports, via secondhand summary, that many Anthropic and OpenAI staff believe some unreleased models are too powerful to release safely.

Why it matters: The governance story is now running on two tracks at once: a congressional inquiry into chip diversion, and an internal debate at the frontier labs about whether their own models should be released at all. Both point the same way — the question of who controls frontier AI is no longer just a corporate-governance matter.

Sources: AI Daily Brief, Latent Space

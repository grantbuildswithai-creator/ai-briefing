1. From safety lab to $81B run rate: Anthropic's unlikely path to the top

NLW's new show *How We Got Here* traces Anthropic's arc: in 2021 it was a safety lab meeting in a park whose founders had ruled out selling anything; five years later it is running at an $81B annualized revenue run rate. The excerpt covers the chatbot the founders wouldn't release, an investor who turned out to be a fraud, and the bet on coding — Claude Code — that changed the business of AI. For scale: Anthropic's IPO prospectus reported $33.2B of revenue in the first nine months of 2026 (Q3 alone was ~$17B), and the $81B figure is a run rate — the last 28 days of usage fees annualized — not revenue already earned.

Why it matters: The coding bet is the hinge of the story. Claude Code turned a safety-first lab into the company setting the pace on agentic coding revenue, and the run-rate math shows just how fast that compounding is running — up $10B in a single month, September to October. The episode is a useful reminder that Anthropic's commercial success was not the plan; it was the byproduct of a product decision the founders once resisted.

Sources: AI Daily Brief

2. Clay's GTM agents: $1,500 to qualify every company on Earth

Clay — the go-to-market data platform that raised $115M at a $7.1B valuation in September — is rebuilding its agent stack around coding agents. Head of AI Jeff Barg tells Latent Space that a classifier could check all 40 million companies in Clay's database against a customer's ideal profile for about $1,500, and that frontier models have been acing Clay's lead-qualification evals for about a year. Usage is already split roughly 50/50 between Clay's own agent and outside harnesses like Claude Code, so the company is building go-to-market infrastructure any agent can use. Its agent, Sculptor, was rebuilt as a coding agent running the Clay CLI in a cloud sandbox, after the first version — a tool loop with a tuned system prompt — proved good at adding columns and bad at booking meetings.

Why it matters: Clay is a live case study in what agentic software looks like when the model is not the moat: the value sits in the data layer and the evals, and the company is betting customers will stop picking models at all — Clay will pick for them. The post-training angle matters too: Barg says training smaller models on Clay's own tools and queries is only now becoming viable, a path from frontier-model dependence toward cheaper, specialized agents.

Sources: Latent Space

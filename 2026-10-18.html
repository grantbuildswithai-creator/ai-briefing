Top stories

1. A new essay claims neural networks were interpretable all along

Former OpenAI researcher Nick Cammarata published an essay being described as a potentially historic AI breakthrough: his argument is that neural networks may already be far more understandable than researchers believed, and that years of apparent complexity may reflect flaws in *how we've been looking at them* rather than real incomprehensibility. The surprising thread: a connection between recognizing cat faces and car fronts — and the counter-intuitive claim that bigger models could be *easier* to interpret, not harder.

Why it matters: If Cammarata is right, the interpretability bottleneck shrinks dramatically — which matters directly for the hardest safety question: detecting deception in advanced models. The essay's reception is still in the "big claim, check the evidence" phase; the important part is what holds up under scrutiny and what remains unproven.

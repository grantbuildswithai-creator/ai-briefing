Top stories

1. Perplexity hits a $1B revenue run rate, up 4x in 2026

Perplexity crossed a $1B annual revenue run rate, up from under $250M at the start of the year and over $750M by August, with its agentic browser Computer driving much of the growth. Alongside the milestone, Perplexity shipped a Monitor API (webhooks when something relevant changes) and launched a new research lab with access to 10,000+ GPUs, planning to publish papers, data, models, and code.

Why it matters: A $1B run rate puts Perplexity in rare company among AI-native startups — at typical multiples that implies a $30–50B valuation if the unit economics hold. The research-lab launch also signals ambition beyond the search product: Perplexity wants a seat at the frontier-research table, not just the distribution one.

Sources: Latent Space

2. OpenAI partners with NATO on allied cyber defense

OpenAI announced it is working with NATO and allied cyber agencies to help trusted defenders find vulnerabilities, test fixes, and protect essential systems.

Why it matters: This is the defender side of the AI-cyber story landing in an institutional form — days after a16z argued AI has tilted the economics of offense (see below), the labs are formalizing their role inside allied defense structures. It also extends OpenAI's government-partnership pattern from individual countries to a military alliance.

3. OpenAI opens free frontier-model access to 200,000 researchers

OpenAI is expanding its ChatGPT for Academic Researchers program to offer 200,000 researchers worldwide free access to its frontier models. In a companion post, "Science at the Frontier," OpenAI reports findings from more than 1,800 scientists on how they use ChatGPT in daily research workflows, productivity, and expectations for discovery.

Why it matters: A day after OpenAI credited GPT-6 Astra with helping define the Standard Model on a lattice, the lab is widening the scientist pipeline that produces such results — and publishing the usage evidence to back the claim. Free frontier access at this scale is also a quiet bid for the research community's default tooling, ahead of rivals.

Sources: OpenAI (article returned 403; summary from the feed description), OpenAI (article returned 403; summary from the feed description)

4. The week ahead: five questions that could reshape AI

NLW's AI Daily Brief frames the week around five open questions: how Anthropic's IPO will land and reset how Wall Street values AI companies, whether OpenAI will release a new model, whether Jensen Huang can return to Nvidia, how far the political fallout from his departure spreads, and whether Congress tightens chip export controls. Nvidia reports Q3 earnings after the close today, with the interim-CEO and investigation questions hanging over the print.

Why it matters: The Huang story has moved from a company event to a policy event — export controls, China hawks in Washington, and the financing behind the AI buildout are all now entangled with who runs Nvidia. Anthropic's debut, meanwhile, will be the first public-market read on a frontier lab's valuation.

Sources: AI Daily Brief (episode page show notes; no full transcript)

5. a16z: the 1990s zero-day era is back

In an a16z essay, the firm argues AI has returned cybersecurity to the prolific zero-day cadence of the late 1990s and early 2000s: models like Opus 4.5/4.6 have made vulnerability discovery and exploit development orders of magnitude faster and cheaper, while patching timelines have not caught up. The piece predicts a return of self-propagating internet worms (now aimed at stealing API keys for inference rather than crypto mining), a shift toward patching at machine speed, and a new generation of startups built around hardened infrastructure components.

Why it matters: The offense-defense balance is the most concrete near-term consequence of capable models — and the essay's worm prediction is a specific, testable claim about 2027. It also reframes the NATO/OpenAI announcement above: defender tooling is racing an offense that is already industrializing.

Sources: a16z News

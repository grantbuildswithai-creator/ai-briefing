Top stories

1. Anthropic expands its GSA partnership, offering Claude at a 40% discount across government

Anthropic expanded its partnership with the US General Services Administration: government partners now get a 40% discount on Claude Enterprise, Claude for Government, and the Claude API — for the first time across every product surface, including direct purchases today and via cloud providers in the coming months. The discount extends to state, local, tribal, and territorial governments, with no minimum spend commitment; Anthropic estimates it will save the government $1 billion. About 500 US federal, state, and local organizations now use Claude, with weekly Claude for Government users up roughly eightfold since February and federal civilian usage up about 20-fold in a year. Anthropic cites examples including a Utah pilot where 71% of developers reported saving 20+ hours, and a state team that responded to a ransomware attack in 8 minutes versus the usual 2–3 days.

Why it matters: A week after filing its S-1, Anthropic is locking in the public sector as a growth channel — discounted, commitment-free pricing is designed to remove procurement friction, and the usage numbers suggest government adoption is already accelerating fast.

Sources: Anthropic News

2. Google DeepMind unveils Genie 4, its most realistic interactive world model yet

Google DeepMind introduced Genie 4, a real-time world model that generates explorable environments users can steer with text — events, camera angles, and actions mid-generation — now with ambient sound, music, and real-time dialogue with characters, a first for the Genie line. It is built on Gemini Omni, with Gemini running in the loop to plan multi-step events and keep scenes coherent, and DeepMind claims improved physics including aerodynamics, collisions, and natural human motion. The research preview is open to trusted testers, with Google AI Ultra subscribers next via Project Genie, followed by early-access partners through the Gemini API and AI Studio — Genie's first API availability. DeepMind positions it as a step toward safe virtual environments for testing agents and robots.

Why it matters: World models are moving from impressive demos toward a training and testing substrate for agents and robotics — and an API release turns Genie from a research showcase into something developers can build on.

Sources: Latent Space (Google AI Blog had no Genie 4 post in today's feed)

3. OpenAI brings Oracle apps and data into ChatGPT

OpenAI and Oracle expanded their partnership to bring Oracle applications and data into ChatGPT, letting users describe what they need and complete approved business workflows — e.g. a finance team member investigating costs and month-end close — across finance, procurement, and planning apps, governed by each organization's permissions and approvals. Alongside it, Oracle announced AI Workspace (vulnerability detection built with OpenAI cyber models) and a small-business bundle combining ChatGPT Business and Codex with Oracle apps and OCI credits. Oracle reports 130,000 active ChatGPT users and 95,000+ active Codex users internally, with recruiting research that once took 2–4 days now taking 15–20 minutes.

Why it matters: This is the enterprise-agent land grab in action: the winner may be whoever's assistant sits inside the systems of record where work actually happens, not just the chat window. Oracle's own adoption numbers are a useful proof point for that pitch.

Sources: OpenAI News

4. a16z: Databricks and the future of the firm

a16z's latest newsletter argues that agents plus new database technology will reshape what firms look like and what people do at work all day. The core claim: Databricks — now seeing more queries from agents than from people, with over 17 million databases launched by agents every day — is building the "system of record" agents need to work inside real companies, make real decisions, and contribute to a company's knowledge base. As agents outnumber people, the piece asks how firms differentiate and create value, and suggests the long-term shift may be less "everyone is an engineer" than "everyone is a manager of agents."

Why it matters: The agent-economics debate usually focuses on models; this is the infrastructure version — whoever owns the system of record for agent work owns a chokepoint, and Databricks' agent-query volume suggests that shift is already underway.

Sources: a16z News · a16z.com

5. Anthropic ends coding interviews: "hunger beats tenure"

Anthropic has ended traditional coding interviews for engineering hires, a hiring manager at the company announced: with coding largely solved by AI, quizzing candidates on React challenges is, in his words, as pointless as quizzing them on assembly. Instead, candidates are asked for ambitious ideas that would dramatically improve the organization's goals, and to build working prototypes — with the warning that junior candidates often cram multiple ideas into one prototype, hiding strong ideas among weak ones. Latent Space frames it as charting the path forward for junior engineers in a world where the interview skill and the job skill have diverged.

Why it matters: Hiring is a lagging indicator of what the industry actually values. If a frontier lab stops testing the skill its own models have automated, expect the rest of the market's interview playbook — and junior engineers' preparation — to follow.

Sources: Latent Space

AI Briefing — Thu, Nov 26, 2026

Top stories

1. The math-proof wave keeps accelerating: OpenAI claims an exact matrix-multiplication exponent

A day after the VP ≠ VNP and Riemann claims, OpenAI researchers say they have now pinned down the square matrix-multiplication exponent ω to exactly 9/4 — a lower bound matching the upper bound the lab claimed in October, via an explicit superlinear circuit lower bound. A separate paper claims matching upper and lower bounds for the finite Fourier transform (n√log n), and another claims a proven NFS-class factoring bound beating a 1992 record. Latent Space counts 64 AI-assisted research advances so far this year — 20 of them in November alone — and notes the tally follows public releases, with proofs still under review, including three October withdrawals after a sign error.

Why it matters: An exact ω would be a genuinely shocking result — most experts expected ω = 2 — and the pace is the story as much as any single claim: landmark results are now arriving faster than the community can verify them. Every one of these claims is still unverified, and the factoring claim in particular invites scrutiny (a Claude-generated analysis argues RSA and ECC are unaffected), but the verification bottleneck itself is becoming the bottleneck.

Sources: Latent Space

2. Thariq Shihipar moves Anthropic → OpenAI — and frames the two futures of AI

Thariq Shihipar, a prominent voice on Claude Code and agentic coding, has announced a move from Anthropic to OpenAI — the second high-profile Anthropic-to-OpenAI move in recent weeks. In his AI Engineer CODE keynote, he lays out two coexisting futures: a "revolution" in which AI makes software cheap enough to produce by the thousand, and a "renaissance" in which the same tools give builders more time to make one thing feel right. The talk covers what changed his mind about coding agents, how a decision model can choose from actions a designer defines, and who decides which experiments deserve the compute.

Why it matters: The move itself is a small talent-market signal; the framing is the more durable idea. As coding agents collapse the cost of producing software, the scarce resource shifts from output to taste — deciding what is worth building, and what deserves compute. Shihipar's keynote is one of the clearer articulations of that shift from inside the labs.

Sources: Latent Space, Latent Space

3. Gemini 4 Neon adoption keeps climbing — and the frontier-model race gets a scoreboard

Google's Gemini 4 Neon ran 44.5B tokens on Perplexity on Tuesday, 23.5% above all of Monday, with hours still to go — a Day-1 paid-API usage record that keeps compounding. Meanwhile the independent-eval picture is filling in: Neon ranks #3 on FrontierSWE at 62.4%, GPT-6.2 Astra leads a robot-control benchmark at 71.0, Fable 5.2 leads a 54-task physics-research benchmark at 32%, and METR notes published pretraining loss is falling ~7x faster since early 2026 than in 2022–25 — though critics say the measurement may reflect data-mix shifts more than capability.

Why it matters: Neon's first week is holding up on usage, not just benchmarks, which is the harder test. And the eval landscape is fragmenting in a useful way: no single model leads everywhere, so the "best model" question is increasingly a "best for what" question.

Sources: Latent Space

1. The 5 debates shaping AI, revisited

The AI Daily Brief's Sunday edition revisits the format of its most popular episode — a point-by-point tour of the five debates defining AI right now, and how the questions have shifted: Can AI revenue justify the infrastructure spending? Is AI a mass market or a power-user market? Do businesses want AI they control, or simply cheaper models? Who should regulate it? And can data centers win over their neighbors?

Why it matters: These are the load-bearing questions under every AI bet this year — the capex cycle, consumer monetization, open vs. proprietary models, the state-by-state regulatory patchwork, and the community-fight over power and data centers. Five clean lenses for reading the rest of the news.

Sources: AI Daily Brief (Oct 11)

2. Standard Bots: what industrial robots teach about AI reliability

Latent Space's latest engineering deep-dive goes inside Standard Bots — "America's largest AI-native industrial robot manufacturer," recently raised at a $1 billion valuation in a Series C led by General Catalyst and RoboStrategy. The pattern: pretrained models adapted to factory tasks through demonstrations and in-situ corrections from real deployments, a zero-shot perception system for machine tending (model handles perception, conventional programming handles motion), and inference kept on-prem — "cloud compute for robotics is operationally extremely hard; most factories don't have reliable internet." Key thesis from their Head of AI: data quality beats raw volume, and no model is truly hardware-agnostic — co-optimizing the model with the control policy is the real advantage.

Why it matters: The physical-AI playbook is converging on a pattern worth knowing: learned perception + conventional control, edge inference, fleet learning. It's also a data-quality-first rebuttal to the "more compute solves everything" reflex — from a company shipping real hardware at scale.

Sources: Latent Space

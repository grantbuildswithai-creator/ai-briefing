1. AI-native companies don't hire "AI teammates" — they organize agents around the work

NLW's AI Daily Brief looks at what actually separates AI-native companies from everyone else, drawing on Stripe, Shopify, Uber, Ramp, and others. Seven practices stand out: shared company context that agents can draw on, work that triggers automatically instead of waiting for a prompt, verification built into the loop, and clear human accountability. The episode's bigger argument is that the popular "AI teammate" framing — a fixed roster of digital coworkers — may be giving way to something more effective: flexible groups of agents assembled around the work itself, then dissolved when it's done.

Why it matters: Most companies are still bolting agents onto old org charts. The AI-native playbook NLW describes treats agents as a capability to orchestrate, not as headcount to hire — and if that framing wins, the org-design question becomes which work gets an agent group, not which role gets an agent.

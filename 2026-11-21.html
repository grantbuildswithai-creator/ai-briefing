1. Riemann Hypothesis claimed proved — twice, independently, on the same day

Two mathematicians claimed independent proofs of the Riemann Hypothesis on November 20, the most famous open problem in mathematics. Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge says he found a proof of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis working with Claude, releasing only Lean-formalized code — about 1.3GB, with a prose version reportedly running past 250,000 pages — rather than a conventional paper. The work began October 6, with the final step landing November 16. Separately, Alessandro Masullo announced a purely human proof, reported as a roughly 940-page GRH draft.

Why it matters: If either claim survives expert review it would be the largest demonstrated AI advance in mathematics by a wide margin — and the contrast between the two approaches is the story: a machine-checkable proof no human will ever read in full versus a 940-page human draft. But "claimed" is doing heavy work here. OpenAI's September Millennium Prize claim is the cautionary tale — critics argued its Lean proof did not match the natural-language result — and both new claims will take years to verify. Lean can confirm the formalized statement is GRH with a bug chance well under 1%, but humans still have to confirm the formal statement is actually the theorem claimed. The Claude model used and the degree of human steering have not been disclosed.

Sources: Latent Space

2. OpenAI moves against adversarial distillation — reasoning-extraction attacks fall from 54% to 0.2%

OpenAI published how it detects and disrupts adversarial distillation: using a frontier model's outputs without authorization to train a copycat model that inherits the capabilities but not the safeguards. The sharpest risk is extraction of protected reasoning — the model's chain-of-thought traces, which make especially valuable training data. In GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol, OpenAI strengthened defenses against that extraction, and in an internal adaptive evaluation the average attack success rate fell from 54.0% against GPT-6 Astra with its original safeguards to 0.2% against GPT-6.2 Astra with the updated ones. The defenses combine adversarial testing, model training, classifiers, API-level protections, and investigations into coordinated extraction attempts, aligned more closely with the safeguards used for biology and cybersecurity.

Why it matters: Distillation is the quiet leak in the frontier-lab business model and safety case: a model that helps defenders find vulnerabilities can also teach an attacker to exploit them, and a model that assists biological research can teach someone to build a pathogen — if the capabilities transfer without the refusals. OpenAI publishing its detection playbook, and a 270-fold reduction in extraction success, is both a defense of its own models and an attempt to set an industry norm before the technique spreads further.

Sources: OpenAI

3. OpenAI launches MedChemBench, a drug-discovery benchmark — GPT-6.2 Astra scores 84.5%

OpenAI introduced MedChemBench, a benchmark that tests whether AI models can do medicinal-chemistry reasoning: reading molecular structures, interpreting experimental results, and choosing which compounds to test next. It comprises 99 problems drawn from 117 published papers and patents, covering 761 distinct molecular structures, and grades structure reconstruction, evidence-backed explanations, compound rankings, and agreement with measured properties. At the highest reasoning setting, GPT-6.2 Astra earned 84.5% of available credit, ahead of GPT-6 Astra at 83.3%, GPT-6.1 Sol at 82.0%, and GPT-5.6 Sol at 58.2%. Selecting compounds and predicting numerical properties proved harder for the models than reconstructing structures and explaining evidence.

Why it matters: Drug discovery is the classic multi-objective tradeoff problem — a compound that hits its target may be poorly absorbed or break down too fast — and a benchmark that grades those tradeoffs together is a better proxy for real lab usefulness than single-property tests. The narrow gap between the top three GPT-6 models, and the big jump from GPT-5.6, suggests the gains here are coming from reasoning depth more than raw scale.

Sources: OpenAI

4. Chinese AI labs warn new US chip restrictions could delay model releases

Chinese AI labs are warning that new US chip restrictions could delay upcoming model releases, according to headlines in today's AI Daily Brief. The warning comes as export controls continue to tighten around the advanced chips that frontier training depends on, and lands the same week Anthropic's IPO and Google's Gemini Neon launch kept the spotlight on the compute-rich US labs.

Why it matters: Export controls have been the slow-burning variable in the AI race — their effect shows up not as a single dramatic cutoff but as delayed releases and re-engineered training runs. If Chinese labs start slipping schedules publicly, it is an early signal the latest round of restrictions is biting where it counts.

Sources: AI Daily Brief (headline summary from the episode)

5. a16z backs Linq, messaging infrastructure for AI agents

Andreessen Horowitz announced an investment in Linq, a platform that lets companies put AI agents in front of customers over messaging channels — Apple Messages for Business, RCS, and WhatsApp — rather than inside an app. Linq handles the messaging plumbing so agents can find products, build carts, and check out in a single thread; DoorDash is using it to let consumers order food through Apple Messages for Business. Linq is an approved Apple Messaging Service Provider. The company started in Birmingham, Alabama in 2020 as digital business cards, pivoted through sales software and AI sales agents, and rebuilt around the messaging infrastructure its own agent was built on.

Why it matters: Agents accounted for a large share of internet traffic in 2026, and the question of where users actually meet them is still open. Linq's bet is that the answer is the messaging thread people already live in — and that the infrastructure layer, not the agent itself, is where the durable value sits.

Sources: a16z

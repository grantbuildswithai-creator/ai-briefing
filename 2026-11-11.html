AI Briefing — Wed, Nov 11, 2026

Top stories

1. Mercor raises $750M at a $20B valuation, reporting a $3B revenue run rate

Mercor, the data vendor that supplies expert-labeled data and evaluations to frontier AI labs, has raised a $750 million Series D at a $20 billion valuation. The company reports a $3B annualized revenue run rate and 5 million experts on its platform, pitching a market of more than 800 distinct US occupations whose workflows models have yet to be evaluated against. Latent Space notes it passed on the story last year, when Mercor had under $500M in revenue — the new round confirms the data layer as one of the fastest-compounding businesses in the AI stack.

Why it matters: The companies selling picks and shovels to the labs keep outgrowing the labs' own revenue narratives. A $3B run rate for expert data — humans doing the labeling, evaluation, and RLHF-adjacent work that models still can't do for themselves — is a reminder that the frontier's biggest bottleneck is still high-quality human judgment, and that the firms supplying it are being priced like infrastructure.

Sources: Latent Space

2. Google ships Gemini 4 Carbon, its strongest model for long-horizon agentic work

Google has generally released Gemini 4 Carbon, positioned as its strongest model for long-horizon agentic work in software engineering, cybersecurity, knowledge work, and science. Argon remains the recommended default; Carbon is reserved for the hardest tasks. It is available through the Gemini API and AI Studio for paid developers, Android Studio, and Gemini for business, with Ultra subscribers getting it in the Gemini app, AI Mode, and Antigravity. Same-day integrations shipped in Cursor, Devin, Perplexity, and GitHub Copilot. On benchmarks, Carbon tops CursorBench at 60.4% on high effort and scores 40.0% on FrontierCode 2.0 (up from Argon's 26.3%), and Perplexity reports it matching Fable 5.2 on WANDR at 40% of the cost — but early users also report hallucinations, overconfidence, and verbosity, and it ranks last of 10 on speed and cost in Roboflow's image tests.

Why it matters: The frontier race is now fought task-by-task on cost and capability, not just on headline benchmarks. Carbon's positioning — a premium model for the hardest 10% of work, with Argon as the default — mirrors Anthropic's Fable/Opus split and shows both labs converging on a tiered product strategy: a workhorse model for everyday use, and an expensive, compute-hungry one for agentic marathons.

Sources: Latent Space

3. OpenAI launches Businesses in ChatGPT

OpenAI has introduced Businesses in ChatGPT, a new surface that lets companies manage their business information, see insights about how they show up in ChatGPT, and turn interest into action. (OpenAI's article page returned a 403 to our fetcher today, so this summary is from the feed description.)

Why it matters: ChatGPT is becoming a discovery layer — the place people ask for a plumber, a restaurant, or a lawyer before they ever touch a search engine. A dedicated business profile inside ChatGPT turns that traffic into something companies can manage and measure, and it puts OpenAI in the local-business and lead-generation markets that have long belonged to Google and Yelp.

Sources: OpenAI News

4. Google and Adobe deepen their partnership, trading AI models for creative tools

Announced at Adobe MAX, Google and Adobe are expanding their partnership in both directions: Adobe's creative and productivity tools come into Google's everyday apps — a direct path from Drive and Gmail into Acrobat, Adobe connectors in Gemini and Gemini Enterprise, and Adobe tools in AI Mode in Google Search — while Google's frontier AI models go into Adobe's apps, including a DeepMind image model for Photoshop aimed at near-real-time editing. Businesses will also get Adobe's CX Enterprise Coworker agent inside Gemini Enterprise.

Why it matters: The two companies are complementary in a way neither can easily replicate: Google has the distribution and the models, Adobe has the creative-professional workflow. The deal is also a quiet admission that neither side wins the "AI in creative work" fight alone — and it puts Adobe's tools one click closer to the billions of people who live in Gmail and Drive.

Sources: Google AI Blog

5. Kimi K3.1 Pro lands on Amazon Bedrock

Moonshot AI's Kimi K3.1 Pro — a post-trained version of the 3T-class open-weight Kimi K3 with a 1-million-token context window, tuned for long-horizon agentic work and fewer factual errors — is now available on Amazon Bedrock, alongside a faster Kimi K3.1 Pro Highspeed variant Moonshot says runs 2–3x faster. It works through Bedrock's OpenAI-compatible Chat Completions, Responses, and Converse APIs, with zero data retention and no operator access to prompts.

Why it matters: Open-weight frontier models keep gaining enterprise-grade distribution. Kimi was already one of the most talked-about open models of the autumn; putting it on Bedrock — inside AWS's security boundary, with no data shared back to Moonshot — removes the main objection enterprises had to running a Chinese lab's model, and sharpens the competition for Anthropic and OpenAI inside AWS itself.

Sources: AWS Machine Learning Blog
